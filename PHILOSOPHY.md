# Investing Philosophy

*A short reminder to read before acting on anything this tool produces.*

---

## This tool is a research analyst, not an advisor

The AI briefings are organized information — not instructions. They tell you what happened
and why it might matter. What you do with that information is entirely your decision.
No algorithm, no tool, and no human analyst can know your financial situation, your risk
tolerance, or your goals. Only you can.

**If you ever feel the urge to act on something immediately because it "seems obvious" —
pause. That feeling is the most expensive one in investing.**

---

## Paper-trade before risking real money

A paper trade is a pretend trade: you write down "I would buy X at this price, for this
reason" without actually spending money. Track it for 3–6 months.

If you can't explain clearly why you'd buy or sell something — including what would
prove you wrong — you're not ready to trade it with real money.

---

## Journal every decision

Use `JOURNAL.md` to record what you were thinking, why you decided what you decided,
and what you expected to happen. This is not optional — it's how you learn from both
wins and mistakes. Memory is unreliable and self-serving. Writing is honest.

---

## The SEBI numbers are sobering

SEBI studies consistently show that approximately 90–95% of individual retail traders
who actively buy and sell in Indian equity and derivatives markets lose money over time.
This is not a knock on your intelligence — it reflects that short-term price movements
are close to unpredictable, and trading costs compound against you.

The goal of this tool is not to help you trade more cleverly. It is to help you
understand businesses clearly enough to hold a small number of great ones for a long time.

---

## The actual goal: 5–10 great businesses, held long

Find companies you understand. Understand why they make money. Check that the people
running them are honest and capable. Buy at a fair price. Then wait.

That's the whole strategy. Everything this tool builds — news briefings, stock data,
screeners, book summaries — is in service of that one idea.

---

## Never invest on a tip. Only on conviction you can articulate.

"My friend said" is not a reason.
"It looked like it was going up" is not a reason.
"I have a gut feeling" is not a reason.

A reason is: *"I understand this business, I've read the last three annual reports,
I know what could go wrong, and at this price the risk seems worth the potential reward."*

If you can't say something like that, you're not investing — you're gambling.
